# Jenga Taifa

### A design white paper for the Sichon Engineering Company website

*Version 1.0 — 3 July 2026 · Nairobi*

> "Engineering is not merely a technical profession — it is a civic responsibility."
> — *Sichon Engineering Company, founding brief*

---

## 0. Why this document exists

Most company websites are brochures. They describe. This one should *do*.

Sichon is not selling a SaaS subscription or a pair of shoes. It is selling the
most consequential thing a young nation buys: **the belief that the gap between
what Kenya is and what Kenya could be can be closed — by disciplined hands, on
budget, without cutting corners.** A website for that mission cannot merely list
services in tasteful type. It has to make a visitor *feel* the distance, and then
watch it close.

This paper lays out the idea, the evidence behind it, and the plan. It is meant to
be read with a cup of coffee. Enjoy it.

---

## 1. The honest starting point

The first redesign was good. Clean editorial layout, confident typography, a
restrained amber accent, a tasteful "blueprint" motif. It would not embarrass
anyone in a boardroom.

But judged against the mission, it had three quiet failures:

1. **It stated the mission; it never dramatized it.** The infrastructure gap —
   Sichon's entire reason to exist — sat in a grey paragraph you scrolled past.
2. **It had no proof and no place.** No projects, numbers, faces, or maps. Swap
   the logo and it could be a design studio in Berlin. Nothing said *Kenya*.
3. **Its aesthetic was "safe premium, circa 2023."** Serif-plus-sans-plus-one-accent
   is a lovely formula shared by ten thousand agency templates. Safe is
   forgettable. And forgettable is the one thing the face of a company cannot be.

A solid B+. This paper is about the A.

---

## 2. The thesis: the website *is* the pitch

One sentence governs everything that follows:

> **Don't tell people Sichon builds the nation. Let them build it, by scrolling.**

The centrepiece is a single, cinematic scroll experience — *an engineer's drawing
that constructs itself.* You arrive in darkness: an unlit landscape, a lone survey
benchmark, faint topographic contours. That darkness is the **gap** — deliberate,
uncomfortable, real. Then you scroll, and Sichon's method plays out as line-work
drawing itself onto the page:

| Scroll | Scene | Sichon's method |
|-------:|-------|-----------------|
| 0–10% | Night. A survey benchmark. "There is a gap." | *We start in the dark, honestly.* |
| 15–29% | Survey lines sweep the datum | **01 · Identify** the real problem |
| 30–47% | A road draws into perspective; a retaining wall sections up | **02 · Design & Build** the built environment |
| 48–65% | Solar panels rise, the sun ascends, street-lights ignite; the sky lifts from night to day | **03 · Power** — the lights come on |
| 66–81% | Trees grow from the datum line; the world greens | **04 · Sustain** the environment |
| 85–100% | A city stands in full daylight. *"Jenga Taifa. Build the Nation."* | The nation, built |

It is not decoration bolted onto a pitch. **The drawing coming to life *is* the
pitch** — because engineering has always been drawings coming to life. By the time
a visitor reaches "Build the Nation," they have not read a claim. They have
witnessed a demonstration.

---

## 3. Why this is the right bet (the evidence)

This isn't taste for taste's sake. Three currents in mid-2026 design all point the
same way:

- **Scrollytelling has matured.** Using scroll position to advance a narrative and
  reveal information sequentially is now a mainstream discipline, not an
  experiment. ([Figma, 2026 trends](https://www.figma.com/resource-library/web-design-trends/))
- **The gold standard for "hard engineering" is narrative clarity.** The most
  admired technical site of the year — Bristol aerospace firm **iCOMAT** — wins by
  translating incomprehensible composite-manufacturing into a coherent visual
  story with a "clarity-first" architecture. That is exactly Sichon's challenge:
  make civil engineering *felt*. ([Awwwards SOTD](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/icomat))
- **Kinetic typography and motion-as-brand are the new baseline.** Type that moves
  and reveals is what separates 2026 sites from 2023 ones. ([Awwwards architecture](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/architecture/))

And for our specific sector, the conversion evidence is blunt: in construction and
AEC, **case studies, real imagery, credibility markers, and location-specific
content** are what turn visitors into clients — and **57% of leads are lost to a
non-mobile-optimized site.** ([OpenAsset](https://openasset.com/resources/construction-website-examples/),
[ProjectMark](https://www.projectmark.com/blog/construction-website-design))

## 4. The gap, in numbers — substance over vibes

Abstract mission copy is weightless. Real numbers give it gravity *and* signal
engineering rigour. Each figure below is cited, current, and maps directly to a
Sichon service — the problem, then our answer.

| The gap | Figure | Source | Sichon's answer |
|---------|:------:|--------|-----------------|
| Roads unpaved | **84.9%** (140,099 of 164,967 km) | [KNBS Economic Survey 2024 / StatsKenya](https://statskenya.co.ke/at-stats-kenya/about/length-of-paved-and-unpaved-roads-in-kenya-bitumen-and-earth-roads/152/) | Roads & Access Infrastructure |
| Without electricity | **1 in 4** (76% access, 2023) | [World Bank](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/EG.ELC.ACCS.ZS?locations=KE) · [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/data-insights/kenya-has-made-substantial-progress-in-providing-access-to-electricity) | Clean Energy / Solar |
| Housing deficit | **2M+ units**, +200k/year | [UN-Habitat](https://unhabitat.org/kenya) · Vision 2030 | Built Environment |
| Urban Kenyans in informal settlements | **46.5%** — 2× the global average | [UN-Habitat](https://unhabitat.org/kenya) | Drainage, walls, public space |

These aren't there to depress. They're the *before* photo. The site's whole arc is
"here is the measured gap — here is the discipline that closes it."

## 5. Honesty as a strategy

Sichon was founded in 2026. It does not yet have a portfolio of completed
motorways. The lazy move is to fake scale with stock photos of other people's
bridges. We will do the opposite, because **Integrity is one of the five values —
so the site must live it.**

Instead of a fabricated portfolio, the site carries an **"Our Standard" manifesto**:
a plain-spoken statement that Sichon is new, that it is *open for briefs*, and that
its promise is process — transparent budgeting, documentation, safety without
compromise. A projects module is built and waiting, empty, ready to fill with the
first real commission. Being new, stated with confidence, is more trustworthy than
pretending to be old.

---

## 6. Rooted in place: *Jenga Taifa*

The single biggest fix to "this could be anywhere" is to make it unmistakably
**here**. Threaded throughout:

- **A bilingual spine.** *Build the Nation · Jenga Taifa.* The Swahili is not
  decoration — it is the emotional heart, and it roots the brand in the country it
  serves.
- **Real coordinates.** `01°17′S · 36°49′E — Nairobi` as a recurring technical
  annotation, the way a drawing is stamped.
- **Kenyan topography** as the contour-line motif — the land itself as the base
  layer of every drawing.
- A **refined** African design sensibility — sophisticated, not clichéd; no
  novelty patterns, just rootedness. (Informed by the current African type-design
  movement — [African Digital Art](https://www.africandigitalart.com/).)

## 7. The design language

**Grounded & architectural**, now with a day/night dimension.

- **Two worlds.** The *gap* is night — deep indigo-black, cold steel line-work, a
  single amber survey mark. The *built nation* is day — warm paper, ink, structural
  amber. The scroll literally moves from one to the other.
- **A palette that reads in both.** Steel-blue line-work is legible on night *and*
  paper, so the drawing never disappears as the sky changes. Amber = human warmth
  and safety (the lights, the mark). Photovoltaic blue = energy. Leaf green =
  environment.
- **Three type voices.** A display serif (*Fraunces*) for gravitas, a clean sans
  (*Inter*) for clarity, and a **monospace for technical annotations** — the
  coordinates, dimensions, and section numbers that make it feel drawn by an
  engineer, not a marketer.
- **A "draftsman" motion system.** Lines *draw* in (SVG path-length), dimensions
  annotate, elements rise from the datum and snap to grid. Motion is the brand
  signature, not a decoration.

## 8. Engineering philosophy (the site itself)

A firm that promises to "execute responsibly" cannot ship a bloated, fragile
website. So the build honours the same values it preaches:

- **Fast and light.** No heavy 3D engine. The cinematic journey is built from
  scalable vector line-work animated only via GPU-friendly properties (opacity,
  transform, path-length). It flies on a mid-range Android phone — which is the
  device most of Kenya will actually visit on. (**Safety & Competence.**)
- **Accessible by default.** `prefers-reduced-motion` is fully honoured: the
  journey collapses gracefully to a single, static "built nation" scene for anyone
  who needs stillness. (**Respect.**)
- **Hosting that never breaks.** Static output, custom domain
  (`www.sichonengineering.com`) preserved on every deploy, served from GitHub via a
  reproducible pipeline. Nothing to fall over. (**Integrity.**)

The medium practises the message.

## 9. The backlog — where this can still go

Shipped in this direction, but held for later so we don't overreach:

- **A living projects map** — as real commissions land, pin them on an interactive
  Kenya map with challenge → solution → measured result case studies.
- **A "Start a brief" funnel** with structured intake (what, where, scale) instead
  of a bare email link — the single biggest conversion upgrade.
- **Impact counter** — kilometres paved, kWp installed, trees planted — real
  numbers that grow as the firm does. The mission, keeping score.
- **A deeper WebGL terrain finale** if the audience and devices justify it.
- **Team & credentials** once licences and partnerships are in hand.

---

## 10. The one-line version

> **A visitor arrives in the dark, scrolls, and builds Kenya's future with their
> own hand — then reads the number that proves why it matters.**

That is the face worth giving this company.

*Jenga Taifa.*
